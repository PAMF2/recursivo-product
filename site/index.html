How it works.

A prediction is worthless if you cannot check it. So we check every one against a real person, and feed what we learn back in.

01

Predict

We give the model a question a real person already answered, and it has to call that person's answer. Not the crowd's. Theirs.

02

Grade

We score the call against what the person actually said. One person at a time, so no average can hide a miss.

03

Segment

We break the score down by age, region, and topic, and show where the model is sharp and where it breaks.

04

Compound

Every graded answer is training signal. The model that gets scored is the model that gets better.

Then it repeats. Each round sharpens the next.

Honest by design.

Everyone leads with a flattering group average. We lead with the number it hides: how often the model gets one real person right. It is low today, and we publish it anyway. The score that grades us is the score that trains us, so here honesty is not a virtue, it is the mechanism.

Individual, not group

We grade getting one real person right, not a crowd average that flatters everyone.

By segment

The score broken out by age, region, and topic, with the gaps showing.

Temporal holdout

Tested on a later wave of answers the model never saw. You cannot fit what you have not seen.

No self-report

Graded on public ground truth, not our own word. No marking our own homework.

Talk to us

Questions, answered plainly.

Vendors claim 85 to 99 percent. Why is real accuracy near a coin flip?
Because we measure individual prediction: did the model match a real person's real answer, on public held-out data. Their number is group-level, the distribution across many people, or unvalidated.
How is a dumb baseline beating the models?
Always guessing the most common answer scores 0.58. The best model we measured, Claude Opus 4.8, scores 0.54. At the individual level, today's simulators add nothing over a constant.
What is the ground truth?
Twin-2K-500: 2,058 real people, about 500 questions each, wave-4 held out (Columbia, arXiv 2505.17479). Public and reproducible.
Will a newer model fix this?
The Columbia digital-twin study ran robustness across GPT-4.1, GPT-5, DeepSeek and Gemini, including fine-tuned variants. All were similarly weak. It is a structural limit, not a model-choice problem a better model solves next quarter.
What is AlphaPrediction?
Our own prediction models, trained and selected against verified ground truth, in the spirit of AlphaFold. The same ruler that ranks others is the training signal for ours.
